It's late again.

The kind of late where the apartment feels like a waiting room nobody's coming back to.
No TV, no clattering dishes, no key turning in the lock. Just the fridge humming in the kitchen and the old clock in the hallway, ticking like it's mocking me.

Mom's on another night shift. I don't even have to check my phone to know.

I scroll through the usual trash on my monitor.
Some streamer yelling at a game I don't care about.
A recommendation list full of **you might like this** from sites that don't know me at all.
Chats that died months ago, still sitting there like abandoned buildings.

I could keep clicking.
YouTube until three in the morning, random games, some meaningless noise to drag me to sleep.

But there's this heaviness in my chest that all of that never touches.

My mouse drifts to the one icon I never uninstall.

The game boots up in a tiny window that the system always tries to resize and never quite manages. The resolution is wrong, the logo is outdated, the whole thing looks like it fell out of another year.

Which is kind of the point.

The screen flashes, neon splashes across the darkness of my room, and the first notes of the main track hit. Not loud, just... familiar. Like a song you've heard too many times to ever really forget.

I don't play this one to kill time.
If I just wanted to kill time, I'd go back to the stream or scroll myself into a coma somewhere else.

This is different.
This is what I put on when I want to pretend there was a **before**.

My hands move without asking me.
**Start.**

I don't even have to look.
Every jump, every turn, every little fake danger on the screen all of it is mapped somewhere under my skin. I could close my eyes and still clear it. And, as always, I do.

No deaths. No surprises.
The level ends exactly, where it should, like it always does.

The victory screen pops up, throwing text in my face I've seen a thousand times.
My fingers automatically shift toward the exit key... and then stop.

There's another line on the menu.
**Story.**

It's been there forever.
Every single time I've ignored it. Clicked past it. Told myself, **Not now. Who cares.**

Tonight there's nothing else in my head.
No excuse left.

I sigh.

**Fine. Let's see what you've been hiding all this time.**

I click.

The music cuts off like someone pulled the plug.
The text appears, leaving some letters on a neon background. My reflection floats faintly on the glass messy hair, tired eyes, that weird too-old expression that shouldn't belong to a seventeen-year-old.

The story isn't about heroes or saving the world.
It's about someone stuck in the same evening, replaying the same day, because moving forward feels harder than staying exactly where they are.

It's a bit too on the nose.

The lines scroll slowly, one after another.
The voice in my head that reads them doesn't sound like me, but it feels like it knows too much. Like it's been watching.

The final lines felt like something was left unsaid.

**Not everything in our lives is as hopeless as it seems. 
You tell yourself that yesterday you could have changed everything, if only the chance had been yours. But if it had, would you really have taken it? 
All the while, happiness lingers just an arm's length away, and still we stubbornly refuse to see it.**

Near the end of the page there's a short post scriptum:

**If you've made it this far without skipping,
maybe you're the kind of person who still believes in impossible things.
Someday, when you really need it, you might get a chance to start over.**

I stare at that last sentence for a moment.

A chance to start over.

Sure.

**Yeah. Right,** I mutter. **Like life has a restart button.**

I hit any key. The text vanishes. No fireworks, no secret sound, no **you unlocked something special**. Just the same old menu waiting for me, like nothing happened.

My head feels heavy, and not just from the screen.
The clock in the corner of the monitor has crawled past eleven again. When did that even happen?

I close the game. The neon glow dies, and for a second my computer the only light source leaves the room almost completely black. Then the monitor flips back to the desktop, washed-out and boring.

I push the chair back and stand up.
My legs complain from sitting too long. The floor is cold under my feet. I walk through the dark hallway on automatic pilot bathroom, tap, water on my face, quick glance at a reflection that looks like it hasn't properly slept in weeks.

**Tomorrow,** I tell the mirror softly. **Tomorrow will be different.**

It doesn't believe me.
Honestly, I don't either.

I turn off the last light and let the darkness take the apartment for real.
In my room, I drop onto the bed without changing, phone face-down on the pillow beside me, screen still glowing faintly.

Eyes closing, thoughts blurring, that one line from the story floats up again:

A chance to start over.

**It'd be nice,** I whisper to nobody. **Too bad that's not how it works.**

Sleep answers instead of the universe.

Everything goes black.
