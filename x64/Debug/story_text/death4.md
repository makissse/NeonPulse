The rest of the day doesn't feel like a day.
More like a long, slow fall.

Classes blur together. Teachers talk, write things on the board, erase them. People whisper, pass notes, scroll their phones under the desk. Every now and then someone glances at me, then quickly looks away, like they're afraid I might look back.

Maybe I should've gone home straight after that first lesson.
But I didn't.
I stayed. Like an idiot.

By the time the last bell rings, my chest feels hollow and too tight at the same time.

I stuff my books into my backpack with numb hands and wait.
Most people rush out. Some hang back in groups. I try to slip into the flow somewhere in the middle, not too fast, not too slow.

The hallway is loud.
Too loud.

I make it down the stairs, out through the side door, past the dumpsters. If I cut across the yard, I can hit the main road and pretend today was... whatever it was.

**Hey!**

The voice snaps like a rubber band.

I know it's for me before I turn.

Behind the gym, out of sight from the main building, three silhouettes peel themselves from the wall. Same comedy trio from earlier, plus two older guys I recognize from the upper grades. Great. An audience.

For a second I consider walking the other way.
For a second I consider running.

My legs don't move.

They close the distance like they're just out for a walk.

**Where you going in such a hurry?** the leader asks. His tone is friendly in the way a knife is friendly.

**Home,** I say. My voice sounds like it's coming from the bottom of a well.

**Already?** he laughs. **But we invited you to our little afterparty.**

The older guy steps behind me. I feel his hand on my shoulder, heavy and casual.
Too casual.

**Relax,** he says. **We just wanna talk.**

The word **talk** does a lot of work there.

It's not a beating.
Not really.

Someone shoves me lightly, just enough to make me lose balance and stumble back against the cold brick wall. One hand pushes my shoulder, another catches my wrist and pins it to the rough surface. They don't have to try very hard. I'm not exactly fighting.

**Hold still,** the boy with the marker says.

The sharp, chemical smell hits before I see it.
Thick, black permanent ink. Cap already off. Tip fat enough to be seen from space.

I twist my arm on instinct. The grip tightens.

**Don't,** the older guy behind me murmurs right next to my ear. **You'll only make it worse.**

The leader smiles, that same hollow thing I saw in class.

**Since you skipped the autograph session after the premiere,** he says, **we thought we'd bring the message to you.**

The tip of the marker touches my skin, just below the wrist. It's cold for a second, then it burns in a way that has nothing to do with temperature.

Letter by letter, slow and deliberate, he writes.

E M M A I S A W H O R E

The same words. The same cheap joke. The same blocky, ugly style they used on the video.

I watch the letters appear like they're crawling out of my veins.

**Now you won't forget it,** one of them snickers.

**There,** the artist says, holding my arm up so everyone can see. **Beautiful, isn't it?**

They let go.

I pull my hand back too fast, bump into the wall with my shoulder, almost lose my balance again. The skin on my arm feels too tight where the ink is drying.

No one hits me.
No one kicks me.
They just stand there, satisfied, like they've finished a project for extra credit.

**See you around,** the older guy says, already bored.

They walk away in different directions, voices blending back into the general noise of the school yard, leaving me there with a burning wrist and a buzzing in my ears.

For a while I just stare at the words.

They look wrong on my skin.
Like graffiti on a grave.

On the way to the bus stop I keep my sleeve tugged down as far as it will go. The ink bleeds a little into the fabric. It doesn't help.

The ride back to the city passes without me noticing a single building. Even the girl from the morning is not on the bus. But in any case all I see, every time I blink, is that frozen frame from the projector and the marker letters on my arm.

By the time I get home, my head feels full of static.

Shoes off. Jacket on the chair. Kitchen light too bright. The note from the morning still on the table like a message from another universe.

I run water over my wrist.

The ink smears a little, but it doesn't wash off.
Of course it doesn't.

For a second I think about scrubbing until the skin breaks.
I don't.

Instead, I dry my hand, pull my sleeve down again, and go back to my room.

The monitor wakes up with a shake of the mouse.
I don't even think. My hand goes straight to the same icon as yesterday.

If there's one place left where Emma isn't a punchline, it's here.

The game starts.
Neon. Music. The same track I've heard a thousand times, the one that leaked out of those headphones on the bus this morning. The one that somehow followed me into real life.

I start the level.

I know this run.
I've never died here. Not once. Not since the very first week.

My fingers move on their own, but my mind isn't watching the obstacles anymore.

I'm listening.

Every little detail of the song feels sharper now. The bass line, the synth, the exact second when the drums cut out and then slam back in. I try to line it up with the memory of that quiet leak of sound from her headphones.

Is it the same version?
Is there an extra layer in her track?
Is the mix cleaner?
Did someone touch it after her?

I lean closer to the screen, as if that will help me hear better.

For a heartbeat, I forget there's even a game attached to this music.

A flash of color hits the corner of my vision.

Too late.

I slam straight into an obstacle I've dodged a hundred times before.

The impact sound is short and ugly. The screen whites out for a fraction of a second, then fades to the familiar **You died** animation I haven't seen in... I don't even remember how long.

I just stare.

I can't remember the last time I died here.

My thumb, acting on pure habit, presses the restart button.

The music cuts off mid-beat.

The screen goes black.

For a moment I think it's just loading.

Then the blackness thickens, like the power went out not just in the room, but in my head. The chair seems to fall away under me. My thoughts scatter, pulled sideways by something I can't see.

There's a pressure in my skull, like someone grabbed my brain in their fist and squeezed.

Sound disappears.
Light disappears.

The last thing that flashes through my mind is the phrase from the story I read last night:

A chance to start over.

Then there's nothing at all.
