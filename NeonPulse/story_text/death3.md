The school swallows us whole the way it always does.

By the time I reach my classroom, the morning already feels like someone hit fast-forward.

I slide into my desk near the back. No one looks up.
That's the thing about being the transfer kid everyone's already decided they don't need, you're either a target or background noise. Today, it seems I'm doing okay as background.

The room fills up with the usual mix:
loud ones in the middle, quiet ones along the walls, those who always sit close to the door like they're ready to run. Laughter that's just a little too sharp. Glances that slide past me without sticking.

The teacher comes in five minutes late, juggling a stack of papers and a thermos. She sighs, mutters something about the projector, and starts plugging cables into the laptop like she's assembling a bomb from parts she doesn't understand.

I'm not really paying attention.

The projector flickers to life, throwing a pale rectangle onto the whiteboard.

**Alright,** the teacher says, already sounding exhausted. **Today we're going to**

The image on the board jumps.

For a second it's the desktop. Then some folders. Then the whole screen glitches, and a video window pops up instead of whatever she was trying to open.

The sound hits before my eyes catch up.

Loud music, badly mixed.
Laughter.
The kind of bass people put under clips when they want to make something feel cheap.

At first it doesn't register what I'm looking at.

Just movement. Skin. Faces.
Quick cuts, too bright, too close. Dark room, bed, strangers' hands. The usual internet garbage kids pass around and pretend is funny.

Somebody near the front whistles.
Another voice starts laughing too hard.

**Turn that off!** the teacher snaps, fumbling for the touchpad. **Who opened this? That's not**

She doesn't get to the **appropriate**.

The camera angle shifts.
And suddenly the face on the screen is not a stranger's.

It's a girl.
My age.
Dark hair, half-shadowed eyes, mouth parted in a way that makes my stomach knot.

And I know that face.
Not from the bus. From somewhere deeper. Somewhere I've locked away so hard that even thinking about touching the lock makes me want to throw up.

But that's not her.
It can't be her.

The movements are wrong.
The lighting is wrong.
The whole thing feels like a mask badly glued over somebody else.

My fingers dig into the edge of the desk. The noise in the room gets louder, like someone turned up the volume on every voice at once.

**Is that holy shit, is that her?** someone hisses.

**Wait, pause it, pause it!** another one laughs. **Look, look, look**

The name lands like a punch to the chest.

Emma.

I know where they dug her name up from.

Emma wasn't some random girl. She was a very a close person for me, also we had close relationship. 

Yeah, we loved each other.

But, anything ended in a couple of weeks.

Doctors found she had acute lymphoblastic leukaemia the kind of blood cancer.

She went through rounds of chemo until her immune system was basically gone, and they moved her into a positive-pressure isolation room  a **sterile box**, white walls, filtered air, no one could step inside. 

The only real chance left was a bone-marrow transplant: best case, it works and she gets more time; worst case, she dies. That what she and her parents told me. 
I never got along with her parents, but now they had no reason to refuse her from seeing me.

A month later she was scheduled for surgery, and all I could do was wait.

Unfortunately for me, that's where the story ended. **The operatipn went badly,** they said. No details, just a quiet line that turned everything about her into past tense.

Apparently someone thought that was good material for a joke.

My vision goes blurry for a second.
Not from tears my body feels too numb even for that just from the sheer wrongness of hearing her name in this room, in that tone, over this kind of video.

I stare at the floor.
If I don't look at the screen, maybe it stops existing.

**Enough!** the teacher snaps again, louder this time. She finally finds the right key combination. The sound cuts off mid-laugh. The image freezes for half a beat long enough for the last frame to burn in.

Big white letters across the bottom of the screen:

EMMA IS A WHORE

A few people actually clap.

The projector goes dark.

Silence falls, sharp and ugly, like the moment after you drop a plate and it hasn't hit the floor yet.

The teacher's face is pale and furious.

**Who did that?** she demands. **Own up right now, or I'm calling the principal and they will check every device in this classroom. This is harassment, and it is not a joke.**

It is a joke, for most of them.

Heads are bent together, whispers flying:
**That's the dead girl, right?**
**Weren't they together?**
**I heard he**
**I heard she**

I don't say anything.

I don't move.
If I move, I might throw up.
If I speak, I'm scared of what will come out.

The teacher keeps talking about respect, rules, privacy, consequences, but it all washes past me like she's underwater. No one confesses, obviously. No one ever does.

The rest of the lesson is a blur of words and half-finished notes. Every time I blink, that last frozen frame flashes back behind my eyelids.

EMMA IS A WHORE

It's not just the sentence.
It's the fact that someone went to the trouble of making that fake. Of digging up her name, her face, whatever scraps of my past they could find, and stitching it all together just to see me choke on it.

The bell finally rings.

Chairs scrape back. Bags zip. Voices swell. The room fills with the chaotic movement of people who get to just... go on with their day.

I start stuffing my things into my backpack, wishing I could fold myself small enough to disappear between the pages of my notebook.

A shadow falls over my desk.

I look up. Three guys are standing there. Our local comedy trio always together, never funny, the kind of group project no one volunteered for.

The one in the middle leans on my desk with his hand, casual like we're friends.

**You liked the movie?** he asks, voice low enough that the teacher, gathering her papers, won't catch it.

I don't answer.

He smiles without any warmth.

**Don't you dare to run home right after last bell today, yeah? Today, we want to... talk, but it's for today, if won't show up the things will get really worse.**

Another one twirls a permanent marker between his fingers, cap already off. The smell of it stings my nose.

**Got something to write down,** he adds, grinning.

They move away before I can say anything, laughing about something else entirely by the time they reach the door.

I sit there for a second longer, listening to the echo of their footsteps in the corridor, the buzz of the projector cooling down above my head, the frantic beating of my own heart.

It was just another school day when I woke up.

Now it feels like somebody kicked a hole in whatever was left of my world and started reaching through.
