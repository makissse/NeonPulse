The alarm goes off. Different day, same punch.

Ceiling. Phone. Crack in the glass.
That feeling again like I've been run through a shredder in my sleep and taped back together wrong.

Kitchen. Note. Mug.

I don't even need to read to the end to know what it says. My head throbs in rhythm with the ticking clock.

I roll up my sleeves to wash the mug.

Both arms are a mess of ink.

On the right wrist, the ugly sentence I hate even without the full context:

EMMA IS A WHORE

Above it, on the forearm, in my own handwriting, packed tight:

YOU DON'T REMEMBER WRITING THIS.
TRUST IT ANYWAY.
TIME JUMPS BACK ONE MORNING WHEN YOU DIE IN THE GAME AND HIT RESTART.
ONLY YOUR BODY GOES WITH YOU.
USE IT.

Below that, smaller:

CHECK THE DATE.
I'M WRITTING THIS ON XX.XX.XXXX
IF IT'S EARLIER NOW IT WORKED.

My chest tightens.

I grab my phone, thumb shaking a little, and check the lock screen.

Different date.
Not by much  just one day up from the date written on my arm.

Whoever wrote on my arms isn't guessing.
Whoever wrote believes this is real.

And apparently, whoever wrote is me.

On my other arm:

GOAL: EMMA ALIVE.
KEEP GOING BACK UNTIL YOU REACH THOSE DAYS.

But just reading her name makes something twist inside, like hearing a song I can't place but somehow miss.

At the bottom, squeezed in:

TODAY: STAY HOME. TEST AGAIN.
WRITE MORE CLEARLY FOR NEXT YOU.

I stare at that line for a long moment.

**IT'S INSANE, HOW MANY ITERATIONS PASSED? WHEN IT ALL STARTED? MY WHLE ARMS IS FILLED WITH TEXT**

Calm down.

Then I text the school I'm sick, throw my phone onto the table, and go back to my room.

The PC boots.
The game icon waits.

I open it.
Neon. Music. That same track that's been following me from screens to buses to half-memories.

I don't even pretend to play properly.

Start.
A few steps.
First obstacle.

I walk straight into it.

Crash.
Death screen.
Finger on restart.

There's a flash of panic and then nothing.

Next morning. Same alarm. Same heaviness.

Same kitchen. Same note.

More ink.

The old lines are there, but now there are additions squeezed between them and around them, like someone has been layering messages over the same page.

IT WORKED AGAIN.
YOU WON'T REMEMBER DETAILS.
BELIEVE THE PATTERN.

YOU ARE FURTHER BACK NOW.
CHECK THE DATE.

I do.

Another step backwards.

I feel dizzy, like I'm walking down stairs in the dark and only realizing it when my foot hits a lower step than expected.

Further down my arm:

DO NOT GO TO SCHOOL.
DO NOT RISK NEW DAMAGE.
YOU NEED THESE DAYS TO MOVE BACK, NOT SIDEWAYS.

TODAY'S JOB:
 FIND MORE ABOUT LININTS OF GOING BACK
 WRITE CLEANER NOTES
 THINK OF BETTER INSTRUCTIONS FOR NEXT YOU

I sit at the table for a long time, absorbing it, letting the words sink in the way memories refuse to.

I don't remember anything about writing all this, but it's definitely from the future me, it even has some predictions of what was written in the group chat for today.

All I have is this:
A growing ladder of black letters telling me I've been here before.

So I stay home.
I write more.

Trying to reduce any possibility of me not believing the text, different facts only I know. 
Different proofs that I am writing this from the future.

I add arrows, underline dates, circle **EMMA ALIVE** three times so the next version of me doesn't skim past it.

Then I go back to the PC, start the game, and throw myself at another death.

Restart.
Black.

It stops being individual mornings after a while.

It turns into snapshots:

Me in the kitchen, reading a slightly different layout of the same words.
Me in the bathroom, tracing old letters, squeezing in new ones.
Me at the desk, restarting the same stupid level.

Each time, the date on my phone is a bit earlier.

Each time, there's a little more writing and a little less empty skin.

Sometimes the ink tells me simple things:

DON'T PANIC.
YOU'VE DONE THIS BEFORE.
JUST FOLLOW THE STEPS.

Sometimes it's worse:

YOU TRIED TO TALK TO HER PARENTS.
IT DIDN'T WORK.
YOU CAN TRY AGAIN LATER.
FOR NOW, GO BACK FURTHER.

Reading that feels like spying on someone else's life through their notes. Except it's my arm. My handwriting. My mess.

I never remember the attempts the ink talks about.
But I believe them.

Because the dates keep falling.
Because my body keeps waking up earlier.
Because every loop makes the line **EMMA ALIVE** feel less like a fantasy and more like a point on a map I might actually reach.

At some point  I don't know which morning  there's a new line on the inside of my elbow:

YOU ARE CLOSE NOW.
SHE'S STILL HERE IN THIS TIME.
NEXT YOU: GO FIND HER.
YOU'LL KNOW WHAT TO DO WHEN YOU SEE HER.

The thought makes my throat tighten.

I put the marker down with a shaking hand, roll my sleeves back down, and whisper to the empty room:

**Okay. I'm coming.**

Then I start the game again.

And fall forward, just to fall back.

One more step toward her.
