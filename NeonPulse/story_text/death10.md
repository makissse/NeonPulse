The alarm goes off like any other morning.

No strange light, no dramatic music. Just my phone screaming until I slap it quiet.

I lie there for a second, staring at the ceiling, trying to remember if there was anything special about today. Nothing jumps out. Just that same heavy, tired feeling in my head I've gotten used to.

I drag myself to the kitchen.

The apartment is quiet. Summer quiet. Just the fridge humming and the old clock ticking on the wall.

I pour some water, turn to put the glass down

and freeze.

Black lines peek out from under my sleeve.

For a moment I don't even understand what I'm looking at.

I roll the fabric up.

Both forearms are covered in writing.

Not a doodle. Not one stupid phrase. Full sentences, arrows, dates, words in all caps. Messy, but it's clearly my handwriting. The way I draw my E's, the way I leave too much space before periods all there.

On the left arm, near the wrist:

IF YOU SEE THIS, IT WORKED AGAIN.
YOU'RE EARLIER NOW.

Further up:

EMMA WILL GET WORSE.
SURGERY. BAD OUTCOME.
YOU TRIED TO CHANGE IT. IT FAILED.
NEW PLAN: GO BACK BEFORE THE HOSPITAL.

The word **surgery** hits hard even without context.
Emma is already in the hospital now chemo, sterile room, all of that. We've heard enough about possible operations to know the shape of that fear.

My stomach knots.

Higher, closer to the elbow:

THE GAME YOU'RE MAKING FOR HER CAN RESTART THE DAY WHEN YOU DIE AND PRESS RESTART.
ONLY YOUR BODY.
USE IT TO GO FURTHER BACK EACH TIME.

I stare.

Me. Writing this.
About some time-jumping game that doesn't even exist yet as a game, only as a folder of not finished code on my PC.

On the inside of my arm, cramped between other lines:

TODAY  STAY HOME.
DON'T GO TO SCHOOL.
PLAY THE GAME.
DIE ON PURPOSE. PRESS RESTART.
KEEP GOING BACK UNTIL YOU REACH BEFORE ALL THIS STARTED.

I check my phone out of reflex.

No school app notifications. No bells today. It's still summer. We're between grades. Emma's in hospital already; I'm supposed to visit her later. That much I remember clearly.

What I don't remember is sitting down and writing these paragraphs all over myself.

Not last night. Not ever.

I flip my right arm over. Near the wrist, in block letters someone else carved there on another day:

EMMA IS A WHORE

I flinch, even though I don't know why that hits as deep as it does.

Above it, in my handwriting, half-smudged:

IGNORE THAT. THEY WERE ASSHOLES.
SHE DOESN'T DESERVE TO BE REMEMBERED LIKE THAT.

The whole thing looks insane.

If I told anyone about this, they'd ask how long I've been hearing voices.

I take a breath, try to think logically.

Option one: this is some elaborate joke I somehow agreed to and forgot.
Option two: I wrote it half-asleep during some breakdown.
Option three: I actually believe all this time-loop stuff is real.

The last one is the worst, which probably means it's the one I was betting on when I had a marker in my hand.

I walk into my room.

PC. Chair. Same old setup.
On the screen, when I wake it, the desktop flickers to life. There's a folder called **NeonPulse** sitting there. Inside  code files, art tests, a music folder. No compiled build yet. No **Play** icon. Just a project.

The writing on my arm told me to **play the game** and **die on purpose**.

There is nothing to play.

I scroll through the project. It's close, sure. A day or two of focused work and it could be something I can send her to try. But right now it's not a game. It's scaffolding.

My eyes go back to the lines on my skin.

THE GAME YOU'RE MAKING FOR HER CAN RESTART THE DAY...

That part, at least, matches reality: I am making it for her. So that she has something fun to tap through in that sterile white box they keep her in.

The rest?
Time jumps? Death + restart = rewind?
That's... a bit much.

I drop into the chair and rub my face.

Maybe I went too deep into my own story ideas.
Maybe I read one too many late-night threads about glitches in the matrix.
Maybe I'm just the kind of idiot who would rather invent a magic mechanic than admit he's scared shitless about losing someone.

The instructions on my arm say **TODAY: STAY HOME, PLAY, DIE, RESTART**.

But there is no game yet.

There is Emma.
Alone in a room full of filtered air and beeping machines, waiting for me to finish the one thing I promised to bring her.

Between trusting some half-remembered madness and doing the one concrete thing I can actually do for her today...

I know which one I can live with.

I go to the bathroom.

The mirror shows me a tired face and two arms covered in a stranger's warnings.

My warnings.

I turn on the tap. Hot water. Soap. Rough towel.

Slowly, I start scrubbing.

The older lines smear first, turning grey. The newer, darker ones fight longer. Some of the deeper strokes don't fully disappear; they ghost faintly under the skin, but the sentences break apart. The arrows vanish. The meanings dissolve.

I stop when, at a quick glance, it just looks like a bit of irritation, nothing more.

Whatever I was trying to tell myself, I decide future-me can figure it out without graffiti.

Back at my desk, I open the project again.

I put my hands on the keyboard.

For a split second, something tightens in my chest  a tiny, stupid feeling, like I'm walking past a turn I was supposed to take.

I push it down.

Emma is waiting for a game, not for stories about time travel and markers.
So I start to work, code filling the screen, thoughts full of her and nothing else.

Whatever went wrong on my skin this morning gets quietly buried under keystrokes.
