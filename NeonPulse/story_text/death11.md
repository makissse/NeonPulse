Sunlight spills through my window and gently pulls me from sleep.

I wake up with my cheek stuck to the desk.

Again.

Neck stiff. Back sore. Mouth dry. The monitor in front of me is still on, the room washed in that pale, late-night blue. My game's main menu floats on the screen:

**Neon Pulse.**
**Play.**

Last thing I clearly remember: deciding, for once, to actually read that **Story** option I'd been skipping for months. Black screen, white text, some weirdly personal lines about chances and restarts.

Everything after that is a blur.

Fragments cling for a moment when I close my eyes:

I traveled back in time by one day.
pressing restart in my game,
ink all over my arms,
hospital corridors,
trying to save Emma,
the same day repeating with small differences,
a younger version of me scrubbing words off his skin and choosing code over madness.

It feels huge and detailed and heavy

Well... to dream something like that,** I mumbled.

Even half-asleep, the logic doesn't add up.

If all my body went backwards in time, why would only the ink stay?
Why wouldn't my hair grow backwards, or my scars shift?
Why would the **rules** of the loop be so conveniently narratively neat?

That's the thing about dreams: they always feel airtight from the inside. You only notice the cracks when you're standing in your kitchen, trying to explain them to a fridge.

I push back from the desk and collapse onto the bed without undressing. My brain keeps chewing on the images even as I drift off:

A chance to start over.
Over and over and over.

When the alarm finally rang, I started to prepare for school.

I sit up slowly.

My arms are clean.

No ink. No arrows. No desperate script about games and surgery and restarts. Just bare skin and the faint impression of where my face was pressed into it all night.

For a second, that almost hurts more than if the words had been real.

**Even in my dreams, I couldn't save her. Pathetic.**

I drag myself through the routine.

Bathroom, cold water, toothbrush.
Kitchen, quick breakfast, note from Mom saying she's on another night shift. Normal things. Solid, boring, safe.

Pieces of the dream keep drifting back and breaking apart:

Why would I keep the same age, same height, same everything, just with more writing?
Why did the girl from the bus suddenly disappear? Who was she anyway?
Why would any of it make sense beyond **your brain needed somewhere to dump all that fear**?

Maybe that's all it was.

A big, messy metaphor for the fact that I couldn't save Emma, no matter how many times I re-run the story in my head. A way for my brain to dress helplessness up as **mechanics**.

And yet.

Her name still sits in my chest like a weight.
The fake video, the way they used her, the way I did nothing to protect her name. What a joke I am.

I once heard that dreams show what a person is really like. Maybe I'd be no different from that coward in my dream.

Stupid nightmare.

I get dressed, shoulder my bag, step outside.

Morning hits like a slap.

The city outside is the same as always: too early, too grey, too busy for someone who doesn't really want to be anywhere.

I take the usual route. City bus to the edge of town, then the special **lucky losers** school bus. And somewhere along the way, despite everything bad tied to him, I'd even started to like him.

The school bus waits where it always does, coughing exhaust into the cold air.
I tap my card, the reader beeps, and I climb in.

Inside it's almost empty.
It usually is.

Dim lights, worn-out seats, a couple of old people in the front, bags on their knees.

I drop into the seat

and freeze.

I feel eyes on the side of my face.

I turn my head.

She's sitting there.

A girl with a languid look, strangely familiar and yet so pleasant.
